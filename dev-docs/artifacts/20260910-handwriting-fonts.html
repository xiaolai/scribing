Open handwriting fonts,
measured rather than described

Every one of the 139 fonts in the catalogue today is Noto sans or serif. Nothing is handwritten. One family closes most of that gap: the Klee One lineage, a pen-written Japanese face Fontworks opened in 2020 and LXGW extended into full Chinese and Korean. Everything below is OFL 1.1, standalone TrueType, and under the 32 MiB ceiling.

Coverage numbers here were taken by reading each font's own character map, not from its description. That corrected two things I had wrong.

Why the Chinese edition matters

The three LXGW editions are not the same drawing at different coverage levels. They print the same codepoint with different strokes, because mainland and traditional standards teach different shapes. For a handwriting tutor that is the whole decision. Read across each row: the GB and TC forms genuinely differ.

Ruled out, and why

Two candidates look right and fail on coverage in a way their descriptions do not mention.

Gaegu

Presents as a Korean handwriting font. It carries 2,350 of the 11,172 modern Hangul syllables, which is precisely the legacy KS X 1001 set. Text outside it falls back to another face mid-word.

Edu QLD Beginner

Pedagogically ideal, being an Australian school handwriting standard. It holds 126 codepoints in total and so cannot set an accented word.
