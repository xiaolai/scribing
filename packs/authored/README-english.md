# English textbook print source

This source contains 52 original monoline manuscript models. Coordinates were authored directly as centerlines; no font outlines, existing SVG drawings, or handwriting samples were traced. The source is MIT licensed. It is an authored practice model, not a certified curriculum, a claim of universal stroke order, or a reproduction of a named teaching program.

The University of Utah Reading Clinic's [Letter Formation Suggestions](https://uurc.utah.edu/general/letterformation.php) was consulted for formation principles and pen lifts. It is a reference for movement concepts, not the source of the geometry or copied teaching text.

Each item in `strokes` is one pen-down movement, including intentional retracing. Dots are independent movements. Do not split a continuous curve at its turns, discard retraced sections, or infer extra movements from the visible silhouette. In particular, A has three movements. Lowercase i and j place their dots after their stems. The h/m/n/r family returns along its stem before forming a shoulder.

The frame is 1000 units, Y increases downward, cap line is 180, lowercase guide is 440, baseline is 780, and the descender guide is 940. `coordinates.xHeight` is the height 340, not the guide's Y coordinate. Optical curves can approach these guides without every bounding-box extreme being identical. All strokes have nominal width 32 and dots radius 16.

Chosen variations include single-storey a and g, an angular uncursive k, a barred capital I, a straight lowercase l and t, a geometric double-v w, and a three-stroke capital N whose two upright stems move downward. Lowercase a uses bowl-first formation; q has a short diagonal terminal; capital W uses a lowered middle vertex. These are deliberate style choices, not claims that alternative manuscript forms are wrong. Stroke plans should stay identified as the supplied model.

Review the complete contact sheet and animations before promoting this pack. Check closed bowls, shoulder joins, descenders, dot separation, A's three movements, the h/m/n/r retraces, and SVG/canvas consistency. A successful source replay only verifies the implementation against itself; it does not substitute for educator review or writing trials.
